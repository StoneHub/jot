# Architecture

One SwiftUI menu-bar application owns the microphone and model lifecycle. A shared JotCore module owns SQLite and the Unix socket protocol; the bundled `jot` helper provides CLI/MCP entry points. There is no web service.

AVAudioEngine → mono 16 kHz float samples → bounded capture queue → serialized speech worker → SQLite transcripts. Resume always listens. Held dictation marks a time range in that continuous timeline rather than maintaining a second recording buffer. Delivery is a separate attempt with saved text and a focus-bound target. A failure to acquire or deliver to a field does not delete recognized speech.

`CaptureChunkScheduler` defines short recognition boundaries. `DictationAttempt` and `DictationRecovery` define saved delivery state and recovery priority. `TranscriptStore` persists both recognized words and attempts. `SpeechService` owns the listening timeline, recognition, and live cleanup, and keeps the members the screens and the socket bind to. It delegates to four objects it owns: `CaptureController` (the microphone, input device selection, and the retry after wake), `DictationCoordinator` (one held dictation from key-down to insertion or recovery), `SessionLibrary` (what the screens read and edit on disk), and `SpeakerRecognizer` (the speaker pass queue and People). The microphone sits behind `MicrophoneSource`; it, model loading, microphone permission, inference, delivery, and the clock are dependencies, so the standalone `JotRecoveryChecks` tool exercises the real service without starting a microphone, socket, or UI.

FluidAudio is pinned at `5c19d5e12320e22bbfb7a1877b089d2665a69add`. Parakeet v3 supplies text and word timing, Silero gates likely speech, and streaming Sortformer supplies four speaker probabilities. A single confident speaker is labeled speaker-1 through speaker-4. Multiple active speakers are marked overlap; insufficient evidence is unknown. Speaker state resets on session changes and audio discontinuities. Labels are scoped to sessions; remembering a voice is a separate, explicit step (see People). Early streaming assignments are provisional in quality; this version does not revise persisted segments when later diarizer frames finalize.

When an ambient session ends (ambient off, End meeting, or an automatic pause), an offline speaker pass runs FluidAudio's OfflineDiarizerManager (pyannote segmentation, WeSpeaker embeddings, VBx clustering) over the session file on its own actor, one session at a time, and stores per-speaker segments and one 256-float embedding per speaker in `session_segments` and `session_speakers`. The pass models load with the live models and are released on Pause; a pass that starts after Pause reloads them and lets go when done. The Pause button discards the session file without a pass. End meeting exports as soon as inference finishes, with the live labels; the pass may still be running. Pass speaker ids are renumbered speaker-1, speaker-2 in order of first speech before they are stored. Once the session's last audio block is recognized, `SpeakerPassRelabel` rebuilds the session's ambient rows from its stored words and the pass's segments (each word takes the segment around its midpoint; rows break on a speaker change or a paragraph pause), and Regroup uses the same segments for a session that has them.

People: `PeopleStore` keeps one row per remembered voice in `people`, a name and one unit-length 256-float embedding that is the running average of every sample saved for it, on its own connection like `SpeakerPassStore`. Saving a speaker name with "Remember this voice" adds the session speaker's embedding to the person of that name or creates one. After a pass relabels a session, `PeopleMatcher` pairs session speakers with people by cosine distance (1 - cosine similarity, threshold 0.65, both FluidAudio's SpeakerManager defaults), nearest pair first and one-to-one; a matched speaker with no name in that session is labeled with the person's name and the person's embedding takes in the new sample. Deleting a person removes only that row. Dictation delivery includes all voices in its marked range and does not filter by speaker identity. The underlying continuous timeline still receives speaker labels.

## Bounds and lifecycle

Ambient speaker attribution uses user-adjustable confidence and minimum-turn duration. Short candidate changes and isolated hesitations retain the preceding confirmed speaker within each inference block; sustained changes create a new turn. This can also merge a genuine short reply. Paragraph pause controls turn grouping, independently of the short capture flush cadence. Dictations may combine nearby same-speaker rows and hide filler-only rows without changing stored source text. Settings persist locally and are exposed by status. `transcript_words` keeps, for each ambient row, its recognized words with start and end times in the session's clock and the diarizer's four speaker probabilities, never audio; Regroup rebuilds a session's rows from them under the current settings.

- Callback-to-controller queue: 8 seconds of mono float audio.
- No separate held-dictation audio accumulation and no 60-second hold cancellation. Recognized text is saved as chunks finish, including while a shortcut remains held.
- Capture flush: at most three seconds per chunk, or a 0.7-second quiet boundary after at least 0.2 seconds of audio. Recognition keeps two seconds of left context and two seconds of uncommitted tail in RAM, then adds the next chunk (at most seven seconds total). Timed-word ownership prevents already committed speech from being appended again. Releasing dictation, requesting recovery, quiet boundaries, Pause, and session changes flush the retained tail. Live updates have a three-second cadence; words near a boundary can wait for the next chunk. This is not a claim of instant per-word recognition.
- Pending inference: at most 40 three-second chunks plus an in-flight job and explicit final barriers. Overload records a gap; a held attempt with a known gap retains its partial text rather than automatically inserting it as if it were complete. An explicit recovery can insert saved partial text, with a partial-text notice; its gap marker survives relaunch.
- Sortformer timeline is bounded to 1,000 frames; local attribution probabilities are pruned.
- Ambient capture starts a new session when nothing is transcribed for the Tuning quiet window (15 minutes by default, or Never). The old session gets its speaker pass at the split; a named meeting runs until it is ended. A session that produced nothing rotates quietly and its audio file is discarded.
- Pause immediately stops the microphone and shortcut listener, finishes saving captured recognition work, then invalidates the generation and unloads models. It retains recognized speech and failed delivery attempts. UI/CLI/MCP remain available. An explicit Pause remains paused across launch; a sleep interruption resumes previous listening intent after wake independently of Keep Mac awake. An input change or stalled input leaves a notice requesting Resume. `ambient-off` is a compatibility alias for Pause. Ending a named meeting exports that session while listening continues in a fresh unnamed session.
- Quit stops the app and socket. In-flight/unflushed audio can be lost at quit; already committed transcripts remain.

## Live cleanup

`PhraseCleanup` groups already-persisted recognition rows into one same-session, same-speaker request. It flushes at a final recognition boundary, a speaker/session change, a gap over 1.2 seconds, or before exceeding 12 seconds or 2,000 UTF-8 bytes across rows. A sentence-ending phrase of at least eight words can flush sooner. An individually oversized row retains the existing model input-limit fallback. Raw recognition stays visible throughout; the capture cadence is unchanged.

One separate live-cleanup worker drains at most eight waiting phrases, without competing for the dictation cleaner's actor. A busy generator gets up to five 200-millisecond retries; overload or invalid output leaves raw text intact. Text-only cleanup can finish after Pause stops the microphone. Installation waits for it to drain; Quit cancels it. Diagnostics distinguish queued work, buffered source rows, and cleanup outcomes without exposing text.

The model edits one joined phrase. Deterministic word alignment distributes its result across stable source IDs, including empty readable text when a filler-only row disappears. `TranscriptStore.setReadablePhrase` commits all derived rows atomically only if every original still exists unchanged. Raw text and timed words remain untouched. No schema migration or retrospective rewrite is introduced.

Live uses native selectable text with a 220-millisecond crossfade only for cleanup revisions. Raw appends do not animate. A selected row freezes the displayed feed and revision until selection clears; Reduce Motion disables the fade. Live's minimum paragraph merge gap matches phrase assembly so fragments remain one readable paragraph.

## Delivery and access

A global event tap observes Fn/Globe or a persisted modifier-plus-key shortcut. Fn events pass through unchanged. Custom trigger key-down, repeat, and key-up events are consumed; unrelated keys and modifier events pass through. `ShortcutTracker` owns the physical-key state independently of capture, so modifier-first release finishes only once and autorepeat cannot restart a recording. The shortcut recorder temporarily suspends the global handler while editing. A focus observer and application identity guard the captured field. AX insertion is preferred; the fallback restores the prior clipboard only if it has not been changed since staging. The app never submits the resulting text.

The tracker uses physical event timestamps. Holds begin immediately. A press lasting no more than 0.2 seconds discards only its tiny held intent; two such presses with at most 0.35 seconds between the first release and second press request recovery. Recovery acquires the currently focused field and selects the most recent undelivered attempt, or a configurable recent speech window (120 seconds by default). It flushes current audio and waits only for work captured before the gesture. All voices are included. Password fields and changed targets remain protected. Failed or unverified delivery remains saved for an explicit retry; Jot does not automatically try a second insertion method after unverified delivery.

`dictation_attempts` stores recognized text, delivery state, and whether there is a known or possible gap, never raw audio. Interrupted attempts with recognized text become recoverable at launch and are marked partial because their unrecognized tail is unknown. Unrecognized audio still in memory can be lost on force-quit; already committed recognition and attempt text remain. Deleting a dictation or session also removes the corresponding recovery records. The window uses word timings when available, falling back to whole overlapping rows only for older transcripts with no word evidence.

SQLite operations are serialized. The application-support directory is user-only and the socket is mode 0600; the server checks peer UID, bounds requests/responses, limits concurrent clients, and enforces input deadlines. A second service does not take over a live socket. Dictation creates no audio file. An ambient session appends its 16 kHz Float32 samples to a user-only file under the service directory, `audio/<sessionID>.f32`, only while "Keep session audio until the speaker pass finishes" is on; the file is capped at two hours, deleted the moment the speaker pass has read it, deleted at once by the Pause button or by switching the setting off, and deleted at the next launch if a crash left it behind. Nothing in it is uploaded. Diagnostics read an explicitly selected existing file.

No model inference sends microphone content to a remote provider. Model preparation downloads model artifacts; future agent reads are separate disclosure decisions. Third-party code and model terms remain their authors' terms: [FluidAudio](https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio), [Parakeet v3 model](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3), and FluidAudio's referenced converted model repositories. This implementation does not relicense those artifacts.

The SwiftUI app uses a single `Window` scene, disables automatic tabbing, and switches activation policy between regular (Dock visible) and accessory (window closed). The menu-bar popover and window share the same service controls. Published model metadata is separate from installed-cache provenance.

## Personal vocabulary

`PersonalVocabulary` in JotCore validates entries and performs one pass over the original recognized string. Matches are case-insensitive literal whole phrases, with flexible whitespace and Unicode letter/mark/number boundaries. Overlaps resolve leftmost first, longest at the same start; inserted replacements are never matched again. Duplicate matching phrases are rejected, including disabled entries. Empty “Heard as” uses the preferred spelling as its matching phrase.

Jot stores the Codable vocabulary separately in its `personalVocabulary` preference. A load error preserves existing data and blocks editing rather than overwriting unreadable entries. Fn captures a value snapshot when dictation begins. The worker saves original transcripts first; the held attempt applies the vocabulary snapshot before delivery. Recent-window insertion uses current vocabulary. The Vocabulary preview uses the same matcher.

## Performance diagnostics

`PerformanceDiagnostics` in JotCore keeps bounded numeric samples, typed lifecycle events, and per-job timings. SpeechService feeds it from the existing sampler, captures lifecycle transitions, and measures job queue/inference/completion time using monotonic uptime. Reports are exposed through `jot diagnostics` and `speech_diagnostics`; they have no SwiftUI view or published view model. See [local performance investigation](PERFORMANCE.md) for limits and measurement semantics.

Jot no longer depends on DevFeedback. Debug and Release use the same product UI, with no picker, overlay, feedback tags, or generated row keys. Historical verification records describe earlier builds.
